## G1: quickening and construction (2026-09-26, the first pass of EXPANSION.md)
The question: does turning a crowded village into a build project make its growth feel earned, not automatic?
- **Quickening** (`villageModel.ts`: `quicken`, `quickenable`): before the stone, four more miracles at QUICKEN_COST (10) prayer each: the thicket's bushes hang full, branches lie thick under the copse, the goats give again, every strip stands ripe; refused while the place is spoiled or already full. Told.
- **Beds and the stakes**: a house has BEDS (2); more people than beds and the village is crowded (`crowded`), and at dawn it sets the stakes for a hut itself (a banner: "The village is crowded (13 to 12 beds)"). She can ask a hut of the stone at any time for HUT_PRAYER (15), one at a time, up to HOUSE_CAP (18) houses. The hut going up is `v.site` (its house id, the wood and water brought, the work done).
- **The huts** stand on a second ring HUT_RING (14) m out, HUT_SPREAD (20°) either side of a founder's house so the paths through the gaps stay clear, doors to the green (`housePlace(id)`; `HOUSES` is the founders' six, `housesOf(v)` the village's now). A worn path runs to each.
- **Building** is the freed gatherers' work: a gatherer whose store is supplied goes to the stakes instead of the stone while the hut wants hands (`siteWants`): fetches an armful of wood or water from the stores ("to the woodpile for the hut", "carrying wood to the new hut"; the woodpile keeps the night's fire back, `storeSpare`), or builds at the stakes ("building the new hut", a tick of work each) once the HUT_WOOD (6) and HUT_WATER (4) are in; HUT_WORK_TICKS (240) of work and the hut stands (a banner). Short again at their store, they go back to gathering. The stakes show it: four stakes, a wall that rises with the materials, thatch that grows with the work.
- **Her hands**: a ring before the stakes takes her stack of wood or water straight into the hut (`deliverToSite`); the tip says what it still needs.
- **Moving out**: at dawn a grown newcomer in a house past its beds moves to a house with a bed free (told), so the founders' children fill the huts; births still go to the fullest house with room.
- **The panel** counts houses and beds, a hut rising (wood, water, built %), or "crowded". The start-over button hides the huts again. Saves carry `huts` and `site`. Dev handle: `houses` (live), `huts`, `site`, `askHut()`, `quicken(k)`, `houseDoor(i)` for any id.
- **For the phone**: whether two beds a house is the right crowding (with it the village builds its first hut on its own at thirteen); whether a hut should cost prayer when she asks it and nothing when the village wants it; whether 6 wood and 4 water, against the copse's 6 a day and the fire's 5 a night, make wood the right pressure (quickening the copse is her answer); whether the huts should also be where the goats' or the field's keepers live (roles are V2's).

## D1: the village lives and dies (2026-09-25, the dark loop's first pass)
The question: do births and deaths, from what the village eats, make the stores and the raids matter?
- **The model** (`villageModel.ts`). The eight are founders; `NEWCOMERS` is a pool of sixteen more (names, places kept, colours), born in order, the dead never born again; `ALL_HOBBITS` for lookups. A hobbit's state now carries its house (`home`: a newcomer's is the one it was born into), its `stage` (infant, child, grown), when it was `born`, and its meals `missed` in a row. An infant stays inside, unseen, and neither eats nor gathers (it is the snatcher's target in D3); a child goes out with the others at CHILD_PACE and CHILD_SCALE of the height; `population` counts the eaters.
