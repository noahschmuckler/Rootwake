# The adversaries: a counterpart for every hero, and the helper who serves them all

Design discussion with Noah (2026-10-22): long-term planning, not a pass. It puts the current game, Hulda against
the mother of goats, in context. Nothing here is built. The heroes are DiggyDwarves' seven as reinterpreted in
`COMPANIONS.md`; the wider pantheon and Delvira first appear in `CONTAGION.md` (the brief). The mother herself is in
`MOTHER.md`.

## Noah's brief

- Hulda is a nature goddess, attuned to plants, somewhat to animals and people, and above all to the
  balance of creatures in an ecosystem. Her adversary is Shub-Niggurath, the mother of a thousand young,
  with her goat-like Dark Young. A forest-and-animal villain against a forest-and-animal heroine is good
  counterplay.
- The other heroes are moderately defined, but their counterparts are not. Stay broadly within the
  Lovecraft mythos, but be open to other mythologies and to wholly new creatures. Prefer entities with
  established canon to contribute to.
- **The King in Yellow** reads less as a patron of criminals and more as despair: the disturbing
  realisation that nothing matters. That is a hard villain to fight, and well matched to the Sage.
- **The Sage** begins in a library, perhaps a library complex. Where Hulda's villagers live hand to mouth,
  the Sage's world has scholars, sages and philosophers who pour themselves into texts, with quests
  for buried libraries and lost scrolls.
  - The King could walk the library halls as a ghostly figure, tainting books and scrolls. A scholar who
    reads one goes mad in some way.
- **Dwarf Fortress's necromancers** as the model. A demon gives forbidden knowledge; the reader becomes a
  necromancer and writes books of necromancy; whoever reads those becomes one too.
  - More broadly, this is a **contagious worldview**: sectors of a future society ruled by ruthless people.
  - **Agrarian form:** someone who corrupts the land and enslaves its people with something like voodoo
    zombie magic, or replaces the living with undead thralls.
  - **Industrial form:** the owners of the Triangle Shirtwaist factory, heartless and willing to harm
    everyone but themselves.
- "Mala" came up as a demon of cities and capitalism, a possible counterpart to the Steward. Read here as
  **Moloch**; see the Steward below.
- **The ask:** counterparts for the Alchemist, the Mason, the Artificer, the Steward and the Smith, each
  with a domain and with gameplay loops that work simply at Rootwake's scale and more abstractly once the
  simulation grows.
- **Later in the conversation:** an adorable tutorial helper.
  - It starts as part of the UI, then appears physically in the world.
  - It is helpful at first, then facilitates the adversaries' introductions and deals.
  - It is eventually revealed as Nyarlathotep, possessing or inhabiting the Eighth, Delvira.
  - Its name or title should be an anagram of Nyarlathotep.

## The rules

1. **Each adversary is its hero's domain with the limit taken off.** Hulda is growth held in balance; Shub is
   growth without end, fertility as a cancer. The villain is a temptation inside the hero's own craft, and the
   hero is the one equipped to see it.
2. **Each spreads through its hero's medium.** Shub spreads along roots and through stolen infants; the King
   through texts. This is the contagious worldview: one mechanic, contagion on a network, with a different
   network per domain.
3. **Each makes thralls that change with the simulation's tier.** The same entity's servant is a village head
   at Rootwake's scale and a factory owner in the society to come. That is what lets every adversary work both
   simply and abstractly.
4. **None is beaten by punching alone.** Each is answered by its hero's virtue practised well, and each cure
   needs at least one other hero, so the party matters.

## The seven pairings

| Hero | Virtue | Adversary | The virtue without its limit | Spreads through | Makes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hulda (Cultivator) | growth in balance | **Shub-Niggurath** | fecundity without end | roots, infants | Dark Young, turned villages |
| Sage | meaning, memory | **Hastur, as the King in Yellow** | truth without mercy: nothing matters | texts, copies, readers | the Yellow (despairing or converted scholars) |
| Steward | coordination | **Moloch** | competition without coordination | bargains, incentives, trade | the Bargained (ruthless masters) |
| Smith | extraction, tempered | **Shudde M'ell and the Chthonians** | digging without restraint | mines, heat | hatchlings, quakes, hollowed ground |
| Mason | shelter, boundaries | **Yog-Sothoth, and the Hounds of Tindalos** | every door is also a gate | doorways, angles | breaches, the hunted |
| Alchemist | transformation with purpose | **Ubbo-Sathla, and its spawn Abhoth** | dissolution without form | water, reagents | slurry-folk, plague |
| Artificer | power, light, information | **Nyarlathotep, as the Showman** | spectacle and power without wisdom | wires, broadcasts, machines | the mesmerised |

### Hulda and Shub-Niggurath (built, in part)

- The pairing the rest is modelled on. As built and planned:
  - the mother as a breach (`MOTHER.md`);
  - her growth as mycelium on the root graph (`CONTAGION.md` C1–C3);
  - the brood (snatchers and sacs);
  - turned villages as her thralls.
- **Her cure is the village loop:** fed, defended, thriving villages are a bulwark. Hulda alone can slow her.
  Driving her back takes the Steward's watches and parties, and later the others' techs.

### The Sage and the King in Yellow

- **The canon** (Robert W. Chambers, 1895, after Bierce):
  - *The King in Yellow* is a play. The first act is innocuous; the second unmakes whoever reads it.
  - The Yellow Sign; Carcosa by the Lake of Hali, with its black stars; the Pallid Mask that is not a mask.
  - Lovecraft and Derleth later folded him into Hastur.
  - Noah's reading, despair and the realisation that nothing matters, is the right one for a game about
    building meaning.
- **His madness takes two forms:**
  - **Despair:** anhedonia. The scholar stops working and their output falls to nothing.
  - **Conversion:** the scholar becomes a herald who copies the play and leaves it where it will be read.
    This is the Dwarf Fortress necromancer: the book makes more of itself through its readers.
- **Simple loop (Rootwake scale):** the library hall at night. A pale figure walks the stacks, as the
  snatchers walk the village, and the pages yellow where he passes. By day the scholars read, and one may
  open a tainted folio.
  - The Sage can spot the Sign without reading the text. He reads the margins, the binding and the marks;
    this is his folio-ordering verb, sketched in `COMPANIONS.md`.
  - Each tainted book has three fates:
    - **Seal it** in a warded vault: it is kept, but the ward needs keeping.
    - **Burn it:** safe, but the knowledge is lost, which is also the King's victory.
    - **Gloss it:** annotate it with context until it is safe to read. This is slow, and it is the Sage's
      real answer: meaning is restored by community and context, not by suppression.
- **Abstract loop:** the copy-and-citation network between libraries, karsts and later universities is the
  King's root graph.
  - Every quest for a lost scroll carries a risk: the find may be Act Two.
  - The research loop on record (texts gathered in the world, finalised at home; `COMPANIONS.md` Part 5)
    becomes a quarantine problem. You do not finalise research on a text you have not screened.
  - Late in the game a whole university can turn Yellow and still look healthy behind the Pallid Mask.
- **The thrall by tier:**
  - **village:** the elder who stops caring;
  - **library:** the converted copyist;
  - **civilisation:** a culture that believes nothing matters, and so permits everything. This is where
    the brief's old reading returns: crime as despair's consequence, not its cause.
- **Needs another hero:** the Mason's warded vault holds what the Sage seals; the Steward keeps scholars in
  community, since isolation is where despair takes hold.

### The Steward and Moloch

- **The canon:** the Canaanite god of child sacrifice. Milton's *Paradise Lost* has him as the fiercest of
  the fallen. Ginsberg's *Howl* made him the god of the machine city ("Moloch whose mind is pure
  machinery!"). Scott Alexander's essay *Meditations on Moloch* (2014) made him the god of the trap where
  every player acting rationally ruins everyone: the race to the bottom.
  - He asks for children, which rhymes with Shub.
  - This is the "Mala" of the brief: a demon of cities and of capitalism.
- **Simple loop:** a Moloch stone offers one village an advantage at a cost. Examples: a double harvest for a
  child; no wolves this season if the warren is salted.
  - Each village alone has good reason to accept. If all accept, the commons collapse.
  - The warren already shared by hunters and wolves (`SETTLEMENTS.md`) is a commons waiting for this.
  - **The Steward's verbs** are treaties, shared watches, agreements over the pools, and enforcement.
  - Moloch is not killed. He loses when agreements hold and wins by defection.
- **Abstract loop:** trade networks and arms races between settlements.
  - Every tech the heroes unlock gives Moloch something new to race over. This is "chaos keeps pace"
    (`COMPANIONS.md` Part 5) given a face: every advantage becomes a trap unless it is shared.
- **The thrall by tier (the Bargained):** the brief's two examples are the same servant at two tiers.
  - **Agrarian:** the necromancer-landlord who works the dead and the land into the ground.
  - **Industrial:** the factory owner who locks the fire doors.
- **Needs another hero:** the Sage remembers what the bargain cost last time; the Alchemist or Hulda restores
  the salted commons.

### The Smith and the Chthonians

- **The canon:** Brian Lumley's *The Burrowers Beneath* (1974). Shudde M'ell and the Chthonians are vast
  worms in the deep earth. Their eggs look like gems or pearls, so miners collect them. They answer
  extraction with earthquakes.
  - They are kin to the mother as revised: a worm beneath the mountain.
- **Simple loop:** the Smith's verb is heat (the underworld study's ore boulders, `ore.ts`), and some ore are
  eggs. Heated, an egg hatches.
  - He learns to tell egg from ore.
  - He leaves a rich seam alone when the ground trembles.
- **Abstract loop:** the extraction economy pays its bill.
  - Mines deplete and the ground subsides.
  - The deeper the karst's workings, the more the brood stirs.
  - Industry trades output against quakes, collapses and hollowed land.
  - The industrial ruins (`RUINS.md`) are the Barrens' history of exactly this.
- **The thrall by tier:** the overseer who sends miners deeper; the mining combine that strips a mountain
  hollow.
- **Needs another hero:** the Mason shores the workings; the Artificer's instruments hear the brood before
  it wakes.

### The Mason and Yog-Sothoth

- **The canon:**
  - Yog-Sothoth is "the Key and the Gate", coterminous with all space and time. Every threshold can open
    on him.
  - Frank Belknap Long's Hounds of Tindalos (1929) live in the angles of time and come through angles;
    curves are safe.
- **Simple loop:** the Hounds track whoever has looked too far. A building with sharp inner corners lets them
  in.
  - The Mason rounds corners, plasters angles and builds apses. That gives architecture a rule and a look.
  - The wards' canonical faces (`RUINS.md`) are all right angles. Containment built of angles is a
    containment with a weakness.
- **Abstract loop:** the subway between the karsts is masonry, and it is the cage around the chaos core.
  - Every gate the Mason opens for travel (shrine-teleport, the deep roots, portals) thins a boundary
    somewhere else.
  - The containment network becomes his to maintain, a budget of gates.
- **The thrall by tier:** the gatekeeper who sells passage; the city that builds ever-taller towers with
  ever-sharper angles.
- **Needs another hero:** the Sage reads which thresholds are thin; the Artificer lights the angle where a
  Hound gathers.

### The Alchemist and Ubbo-Sathla

- **The canon:** Clark Ashton Smith's *Ubbo-Sathla* (1933) is the formless protoplasm all life came from and
  will return to. In Smith's *The Seven Geases*, Abhoth is the source of uncleanliness, endlessly budding
  malformed spawn.
  - Together they are alchemy's prima materia gone wrong: the dissolving half of solve et coagula with no
    binding half.
- **Simple loop:**
  - **A universal solvent:** it works for every recipe and slowly dissolves whoever uses it.
  - **Fouled springs and reagents.**
  - **Medicine against the slurry-plague of Abhoth's spawn.**
  - The plateau's two vision regimes are already the Alchemist's axis (`COMPANIONS.md` 2.1).
- **Abstract loop:** water is the network: rivers, wells, the central lake, the rivers through the villages
  (`PLATEAU.md`).
  - It becomes a public-health simulation: quarantine, clean water, cures with side effects.
- **The thrall by tier:** the quack selling the solvent; the industry that dumps into the river.
- **Needs another hero:** Hulda's living filters (reed beds, the bog) clean water the Alchemist cannot; the
  Steward enforces the quarantine.

### The Artificer and the Showman

- **The canon:** in Lovecraft's prose poem *Nyarlathotep* (1920), he comes out of Egypt as a showman
  exhibiting instruments of glass and metal and strange electricity. The crowds who watch go mad and wander
  into the ruined night.
- **Simple loop:** a lamp that works too well. Light against the night raids is the Artificer's gift, but
  this light draws people out of their houses at night to stare.
- **Abstract loop:** wires and broadcasts are the network. It is mass spectacle and power without wisdom;
  the mesmerised serve whatever the machine says.
- **The thrall by tier:** the lamp-watcher; the city that cannot look away.
- **The Artificer's counterpart is not independent.** The Showman is one of Nyarlathotep's thousand masks,
  and Nyarlathotep is behind everything else in this document; see Petal below.
  - The Artificer's light in magical darkness (DD's traversal key for him) is what finally shows the
    helper's true shadow.
  - If an independent counterpart is wanted instead, the Mi-Go (the Fungi from Yuggoth, surgeons who keep
    brains in cylinders) are the mythos' technologists.

## One engine, seven networks

The contagion model (`contagionModel.ts`: germs and hyphae on the root graph, a daily step, nourishment and
irritants) is the first instance of a general mechanic. Each adversary is the same engine on its own graph:

| Adversary | Graph | Nourished by | Irritated by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shub | roots | villages, infants | woken wards, groves |
| King in Yellow | copies and citations | readers, isolation | glosses, communities of scholars |
| Moloch | bargains and trade | defection | kept treaties |
| Chthonians | mine galleries | heat, depth | shoring, restraint |
| Yog-Sothoth | doors and gates | angles, opened gates | curves, sealed gates |
| Ubbo-Sathla | water | fouled reagents | filters, quarantine |
| Nyarlathotep | wires, broadcasts | spectacle | wisdom, the Sage's context |

Build the engine once and re-skin the graph: this is how the later society simulation stays tractable.

## Petal, the helper

### The name

**Thornya Petal**, called Petal: *A THORNY PETAL* is an exact anagram of **NYARLATHOTEP** (A×2, T×2, and N,
Y, R, L, H, O, E, P once each). It hides in plain sight.
- She is a flower, which suits Hulda's helper.
- The thorns are a joke at first and a warning in hindsight.
- Her first hint can be "Mind the thorns!"

### Her arc

1. **Part of the UI.** Petal is the tutorial: the hint bubble, the tip line (`village.ts`'s `tip`), the "?"
   button.
   - She is drawn as a tiny flower-sprite in the HUD's corner, bobbing, with two dot eyes.
   - She teaches the rings, the stores, the strike.
   - Nothing in her is in the world yet; the player files her with the menus.
2. **In the world.** One day she steps out of the HUD: the bubble's corner sprite flutters off the screen
   and lands on Hulda's shoulder, a petal-winged imp.
   - She is a genre staple, in the line of Navi, Tatl, Fi and Paimon. She points out quests, translates
     the snerks, finds the way in.
   - She is truly helpful, and the player grows fond of her.
3. **The introducer.** As the world opens, Petal is the one who brings each adversary's offer, helpfully
   and accurately:
   - **For the Steward:** she explains the Moloch stone's terms.
   - **For the Sage:** she finds the lost scroll, and it is Act Two.
   - **For the Smith:** she points out the finest gem in the seam.
   - **For the Mason:** she knows a shortcut door.
   - **For the Alchemist:** she has a recipe for the solvent.
   - **For the Artificer:** she brings the wonderful lamp.
   - Her advice is never a lie, only incomplete. Every deal is truly good in the short term.
4. **The tells**, for the attentive:
   - the anagram;
   - her shadow, which under the Artificer's light is not a flower's;
   - she knows of things before they happen;
   - she calls Shub "auntie" (in the mythos Nyarlathotep is the Outer Gods' messenger and Shub's herald);
   - she never comes into the taint, and has an excuse each time.
5. **The reveal.** Petal is Nyarlathotep's smallest mask, the Crawling Chaos as a crawling flower. She is the
   part of him that walked out of Delvira to whisper, and at the reveal she goes back in.

### What she is for, mechanically

- **Every deal is tracked, unseen** (Petal's ledger). Each bargain taken feeds Delvira. This makes the
  brief's "Delvira growing in strength just as Hulda and her companions do" literal: her strength at the
  end is the sum of the shortcuts the player took.
- **Refusing Petal is always a viable path:** slower and harder, never closed. Taking her offers is never
  punished at once. The game never says which is right; the ending does.
- **After the reveal the UI is suspect.** Use this sparingly. One or two hints that are now subtly wrong is
  a memorable betrayal; a hint system that lies throughout is only frustrating.

## Delvira, the Eighth

- **From the brief** (`CONTAGION.md`): Delvira is Hulda's counterpart and daughter, growing in strength as
  Hulda and her companions do. She is the final boss, to be pacified by the might of a united world.
- **Now:** Nyarlathotep possesses or inhabits her, and Petal is the mask through which he works on Hulda.
- **Reconciled with DiggyDwarves' canon:**
  - In DD the Eighth is the Dark Dwarf. He took all the seven's chaos into himself and flung them into
    stone-sleep in the cube-vertex blast. He "may act as Nyarlathotep".
  - For Rootwake, Delvira is the Eighth, and the Keystone is her tower.
  - Taking the chaos into herself is why she is open to possession.
  - The seven start diminished because she holds what was taken from them.
- **Pacified, not slain.** The ending draws Nyarlathotep out of Hulda's daughter. A world that kept its
  treaties, glossed its texts, shored its mines, curved its corners, cleaned its water and looked away from
  the Showman is a world with nothing for him to feed on.
  - "The might of a united world population" is then a measure, not a cutscene: how many of the seven
    networks are healthy.

## The wider pantheon (from the brief), placed

The brief also names entities with no hero to pair. They fit as population-scale adversaries of the surface
and the later game:
- **Cthulhu** disturbs the dreams of the citizenry. Dreams are a network too, and the Sage's dreamscape delve
  (DD's dementia setpiece, `COMPANIONS.md`) is its way in.
- **Dagon's cult** and the deep ones choke overseas commerce. Moloch's trade graph, at sea.
- **The Elder Things** and their shoggoth servants, with inscrutable motives. Builders, so perhaps the
  Mason's rivals rather than his enemy: a civilisation that did all of this before and fell.
- **The King in Yellow** was "inspiring the criminals and thieves" in the brief. It is revised here to
  despair, with crime as its consequence.

## Open, for Noah

1. **One mind or many?** Is Nyarlathotep behind every adversary, as the Petal arc implies, or only behind the
   deals, with the seven powers otherwise independent and sometimes competing? Competing gods would let a
   player set one against another, for example Moloch's bargains against Shub's fecundity.
2. **The King in Yellow's simple loop** is the hardest to make playable and needs prototyping first: the
   library at night, the Sign spotted, seal, burn or gloss.
3. **Petal's first appearance in the world.** Which moment of the current game should she step out of the HUD
   at? A candidate: the steward's waking, the first time Hulda is not alone.
4. **The ledger's visibility.** Fully hidden, or hinted at (the Keystone glowing a little brighter with each
   deal)?
5. **The Artificer's own counterpart:** the Showman mask as above, or the Mi-Go, leaving Nyarlathotep only
   behind Petal.
