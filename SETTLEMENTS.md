# Settlements: the village building tree and the path to it

Noah's brief (2026-09-27, `COMPANIONS.md` Parts 4 and 5): the heroes are the source of the villages'
growth; the steward comes first, connected to hunting ecology; the sage researches and the mason
builds; the building tree should "steal blatantly" from Master of Magic's settlement tree as a
framework, and whenever the next building or function is unclear, refer to it. This file is that
framework (§2), the rules taken from it (§3), Rootwake's tree drawn on it (§4), the pooled ecology
(§5), chaos keeping pace (§6), and the first sprints (§7). Nothing here is built; each sprint gets a
branch, a route and a handoff as every study has.

## 1. The rule the tree is built on

A village building needs three things, each from a different system:

1. **Knowledge**: a hero knows it. The mason knows stone; the sage's research unlocks the advanced
   forms; the smith, artificer and alchemist bring the industrial versions later.
2. **Hands**: a villager trained and assigned to it. The steward's board is where jobs are given;
   without him the village chooses its own jobs by need, as it does today.
3. **Stores**: the resources, from the village's own pools (wood, water, grain, meat, stone).

A building with an upkeep costs it in the same terms (a villager's time, a store per day), so nothing
stands "just because." Once a knowledge is finalized it is buildable at any village that has the hands
and the stores, which is what makes a hero's work compound across villages.

## 3. What its shape teaches (the rules to steal)

- **Free foundations, then ladders.** Four or five buildings cost nothing to unlock; every branch
  above them is a ladder of three or four; the top rungs need two branches at once (the Bank wants the
  University; the Oracle wants the Parthenon and the University; the War College wants the Armorers'
  Guild and the University; the Fantastic Stable wants war and faith). Cheap starts, and depth that
  costs breadth. In Rootwake the "two branches" will usually be two heroes.
- **Terrain gates.** The Sawmill wants a forest, the Miners' Guild hills, the ships a coast. A village's
  land decides its tree. Rootwake's villages already have a land of sites (thicket, stream, copse,
  field, pen) and the karst's stone; the warren (§5) is one more.
- **Upkeep is real.** Every building above the foundations costs gold each turn. Rootwake's currency
  for upkeep is a store per day or a villager's time.
- **Buildings unlock units.** MoM's Barracks line unlocks soldiers, its Stables cavalry. Rootwake's
  equivalent is jobs: a watch post makes guards possible, a hunters' lodge hunters. The steward assigns
  the job; the building is what makes it a job.
- **Faith buildings reduce unrest.** Rootwake already has prayer and the stone (W1); its faith line
  raises what a village's prayer can be spent on and how far the shrine reaches.
- **Housing and Trade Goods are what a town does when there is nothing to build.** Rootwake's
  quickening is that: the land's own yield, hurried.

The MoM branches Rootwake does not take: gold (there is no money; trade between villages, if it
comes, is stores carried by parties), the sea (no coast in the world yet), and the wizard's seat
(Hulda's is the karst). When a next building is unclear, read down MoM's column for the branch and
ask what the villagers would visibly do in it.

## 5. The ecology, simplified: pools

Every creature eats from a pool; every pool regrows by the day to a cap; a pool has a place in the
world where its stock is seen. This is already the village's rule (`BERRY_CAP`, `BRANCHES_PER_DAY`,
`MILK_PER_DAY`, the five strips). The change is to give the dens the same rule.

- **The warren.** Between each den and the village it comes down on, a warren: a pool of rabbits (a
  cap, a daily regrowth, rabbits seen as small figures among burrows, on the map once found). Both
  the village's hunters (§7, S3) and the den's wolves visit it.
- **Wolves eat from the warren first.** A pack out at dusk goes to its warren and eats a meal a wolf;
  fed, it goes home. Only a warren too thin to feed the pack sends it on to the village: the pens
  first, then the villagers, as `VILLAGERS.md` always said ("hunt game, then livestock, then the
  timid"). Regular wolves never overhunt: a pack that finds the warren thin two nights running shrinks.
- **A fed pack grows.** Days of full meals raise the pack by one, to a cap; hunger lowers it. So
  quickening a warren (Hulda's existing miracle, on a new place) fills it with rabbits and, left
  alone, breeds wolves. The village's hunters taking the same rabbits is the balance the player
  reads: the equilibrium number gets a second term.
- **Corruption removes the hunger rule** (§6): dire wolves kill without eating and ignore the warren;
  a poisoned warren breeds rabbits that bite.

## 6. Chaos keeps pace

When a hero is released or a knowledge is finalized, chaos gains the same tech or a counter to it. The
counter is content with a visible difference, never a hidden multiplier, and it ships in the same
sprint as the gain or the one after, so no gain stands unanswered for long:

| The gain | The counter |
|---|---|
| The steward stands with her (guards, parties) | one den's pack turns dire: black, red-eyed, kills game, stock and people without eating, ignores the warren, does not flee when fed; slain, the den's next pack is ordinary |
| Hunting and the warren | the Dark Young poison a warren: its rabbits turn vicious and bite the hunters; purify cleanses it |
| The granary | Dark Young learn to penetrate it; the advanced granary (the sage's research) holds them again |
| The palisade | raiders that climb; the wall (mason) answers |
| The smokehouse | the Dark Young follow the smell from further off |
| The lorehouse | texts stolen by the rival's agents |
| The road | ambushes on the road; escorts matter |

## 7. The build pathway: the first sprints

The order Noah picked: the steward first, connected to hunting ecology, building toward the mason and
the sage. Each sprint answers one question on the phone. S1 needs no hero and is the cheapest; it
gives S2 and S3 something to organize.

### S1. The warren and the wolves' hunger

*(Built 2026-09-27; see VILLAGE_HANDOFF.md "S1". Decided in the building: the pool lives in the den's record, as the pack does; a wolf that has eaten anything, rabbit, goat or bite, goes home, so a bite is once a wolf a night; a night jumped over is not a hungry one; the ruins keep clear of the warrens; the first village's calm ground has no warren yet.)*

*Question: does a pack that eats from a pool the village can see, and only raids when it is thin, make
the wolves read as an ecology rather than a timer?*

- **Model** (`overworldModel.ts`, `villageModel.ts`): `warrens(seed)`: one per den, placed on the
  den-to-village line a third of the way from the den, off the line by the seed, clear of the forest
  and the ruins; a pool `rabbits` with `WARREN_CAP` and `WARREN_REGROW` a day, saved with the den
  records. Wolves: at `WOLF_TICK` the pack goes to the warren; each wolf eats `WOLF_MEAL`; a pack the
  warren cannot feed goes on to the village as today; a pack fed `PACK_GROW_DAYS` running gains a wolf
  up to `PACK_CAP`; two thin nights lose one. `quicken` accepts a warren (the same cost pattern as a
  village site; whether it is paid in prayer or clarity is an open call, since a warren is nobody's).
- **World** (`chunkWorld.ts`, `denWorld.ts`): burrows and rabbits at the warren (standees, the sprite
  pattern); rabbits fewer as the pool falls; the pack seen eating there at dusk; the warren on the map
  once walked past, named by the keeper's rumor ("rabbits run to the …").
- **Reuse**: the den field, the pack model, quicken, rumors, the map marks.
- **Tests**: a fed pack never reaches the village; a thin warren sends it; a quickened warren grows
  the pack within a week; hunters (S3) and wolves draw from one number.
- **Open**: the warren's numbers; whether the first village's ground (calm by design) gets a warren
  without a den.

### S2. The steward wakes; the jobs board; guards

*Question: does putting a hero in a village, and assigning its people through him, make the village
feel organized rather than managed, and does his walking make sending him a decision?*

- **Where he sleeps.** The cheapest true answer: a new overworld place kind, the moot (a ring of stone
  seats round a fallen chair), placed by the seed within the villages' walk, told of by the elder
  ("there is an old meeting place to the …"). He is woken by a channelling of clarity in the ring, the
  sanctify pattern. (The DD-shaped answer, a karst of his own reached by the deep roots at a tier, is
  the bigger world; see `COMPANIONS.md` Part 4's questions.)
- **The hero** (`heroModel.ts`, pure): id, position, the village he is at or the place he is walking
  to, a walking pace slower than root travel, a path over the land avoiding water and the dark
  forest; a save. Directed from the map: tap a village, "send the steward." He walks in sim time,
  visible as a figure on the X Bot skeleton, his own clothes.
- **The board.** In the village panel where he stands: every grown villager, their trait line (the
  "small differences" V0 gave them), and a job to assign: gather (the default), build (G1's site),
  guard, and from S3 hunt and from S4 search. Where he is not, the village keeps choosing its own jobs
  as today, and jobs he assigned hold until need overrides them.
- **Guards.** By night a guard stands at the green's edge on the bearing of the nearest den, with a
  stick; a raider within reach is struck (`GUARD_DMG`, a fraction of Hulda's strike) and scared
  (`WOLF_SCARE` exists); a guard can be bitten and carries the incident. Two guards watch two
  bearings.
- **Reuse**: the village panel, incidents and the compass, the hedge's raider handling, the Mixamo
  clips, the ruin's channelling.
- **Tests**: he walks and arrives; the board assigns and holds; a guard meets a wolf; jobs revert
  where he is not.
- **Open**: can he be bitten on the road (recommend yes, a real cost to sending him; he cannot die,
  he lies up a day); whether his board also reassigns the keeper and the elder (recommend no).

### S3. Hunting

*Question: does quarry shared with the wolves make hunting a judgement rather than a faucet?*

- **The tool.** A sharpened stick: a hunter takes one wood from the store and spends a session at the
  copse; the spear is carried (the plateau's hand axe recipe is the model; the toolmaker's building
  comes with the smith).
- **The job.** A hunter walks to the warren by day, takes rabbits at `HUNT_TICKS` a piece, carries
  meat home (a new store `meat`, a meal of meat counting `MEAT_MEAL` of a meal); the warren's pool is
  the same number the wolves eat from. A drying rack (sticks, no hero needed beyond the steward's
  say) keeps meat from spoiling `MEAT_KEEP_DAYS`.
- **The balance.** The village panel's balance gains the warren; the keeper's rumor says when it runs
  thin; wolves answer a thin warren by coming to the pens (S1), so over-hunting is read in wolf
  tracks by the goats.
- **Reuse**: the gather-and-carry loop, the stores, the balance readout, S1's pool.
- **Tests**: hunting drains the pool the wolves read; over-hunting brings the pack; the rack keeps
  meat; no hunter without a spear.

### S4. Parties, search parties, and the first counter

*Question: does sending villagers out in twos and threes make the world beyond the meadow theirs as
well as hers, and does the dire pack make the steward's gift cost something?*

- **Parties.** The board groups hunters into a party of two or three; larger quarry (boar, from G3a's
  "boars later") needs a party; a lone hunter at a boar is hurt.
- **Search parties.** Two villagers assigned to search walk out on a rumor's bearing and back over a
  day or two, and the place is found and marked on her map as if she had walked there (G2's hints
  resolve). This is the mechanism the worked quest (`COMPANIONS.md` Part 5) begins with.
- **The counter.** With the steward stood with her, one den's pack turns dire (§6). Guards, the hedge
  and her strike answer it; slain in its den, the next pack is ordinary.
- **Tests**: a party's yield and risk; a search party finds a ruin; the dire pack ignores the warren
  and is answered.

### S5. The mason (sketched)

Stone as a store (the karst's foot, a quarry place); the mason woken at a ruin or a quarry; his verb
on the board finishes a site the village raises; the granary, the stone house, the well, the oven and
the mill in MoM's order (§4); the Dark Young's counter on the granary.

### S6. The sage's karst and the first research (sketched)

A library-karst reached by the deep roots at a tier; research as time spent there, faster with the
lorehouse's texts; the worked quest end to end: his finding → the search party → the dig (the den
generator with a ruin's skin) → the monsters and the reseal → the delve with warriors and heroes → the
scrolls → the escort home by a party → the advanced granary buildable everywhere.

## 8. Open calls, answered (Noah, 2026-09-27)

- **The steward's sleeping place**: a moot within the village's walk for now; his own karst later.
- **Quickening a warren**: clarity, no prayer.
- **Heroes on the road**: can be hurt, never killed.
- **Meat**: a high-satiety meal. **Goats are quarry before villagers**: a pack that reaches the village
  takes a goat before it bites anyone. Quicken at the pen replenishes the goats first; a second quicken
  fills the milk.
- **Guards fight**, with the wolf bite mechanics as they are, and may die. That is the incentive to equip
  and train them, and the foundation of the mobile fighting groups to come.
- **The lorehouse** cannot exist before the sage is active; it has no purpose without him. Research takes
  time; lorehouses with dedicated villager librarians or loremasters speed the sage's research.
